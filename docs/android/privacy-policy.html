Sreva Privacy Policy

Last architecture review: 16 September 2026. This source describes the currently implemented local-first Android v1 behavior; it does not claim that planned future sync features are already active.

Core privacy rule: Sreva is designed so your reproductive-health history is not a developer-readable health database. In the current Android v1 runtime, core cycle data, symptoms, notes, predictions, insights and reminder planning are stored or processed on your device.

Data Sreva can store locally

Depending on what you choose to log, Sreva can store period dates and flow, symptoms, mood and pain, temperature, weight, exercise, water, medications and supplements, fertility observations, pregnancy or ovulation test observations, sexual-activity observations, private notes, app preferences, predictions and reminder settings. These records are optional unless needed for a feature you choose to use.

How local health data is used

Sreva uses local records to show history, estimate future period timing, schedule local reminders, generate observational insights, create reports you request, and support local natural-language logging. Sreva v1 does not use this information to diagnose disease, make treatment decisions or claim contraceptive effectiveness.

Developer collection and analytics

The currently implemented Android v1 release does not send your reproductive-health records to a Sreva-operated health-sync server. It includes no advertising SDK, behavioral analytics SDK, remote session replay or developer health-payload telemetry. App stores and operating-system services may independently process technical information under their own terms.

Health Connect and Apple Health

Platform health integration is optional. Sreva requests access only after you choose it and only for supported categories required by the feature. Imported records keep source information to reduce duplicates. You can revoke platform permissions in operating-system settings. Platform health stores are integrations, not Sreva's authoritative local vault. Direct HealthKit access is an iOS-native capability; direct Health Connect access is an Android-native capability.

Sharing and reports

In the current Android v1 release, a doctor report, partner summary, QR code or CycleVault leaves Sreva only after an explicit user action. Private categories are excluded from partner sharing and sensitive report categories require explicit selection.

Backups

CycleVault backups are created and encrypted locally with a user-chosen recovery passphrase. Sreva does not hold a recovery copy or the recovery passphrase for the current CycleVault flow. You choose the destination through the operating system. Losing the passphrase can make a backup unrecoverable.

Security

Sreva uses encrypted local storage, device-protected key storage, optional app locking, private notification modes, sensitive-screen protections where supported, authenticated encrypted CycleVault backups, and release-time dependency and privacy checks. No security mechanism can make a lost or compromised device risk-free.

Retention and deletion

Local records remain until you edit or delete them, wipe Sreva data, uninstall the app, or restore another validated backup. Individual observations and period records can be deleted. Privacy Center provides a full local-health-data wipe. The current Android v1 runtime has no Sreva health-sync database from which deleted local records can be recovered.

Diagnostics

User-generated diagnostic reports contain operational information such as app version, operating-system version, database schema, permission state and integrity status. They are designed not to include period dates, symptoms, notes, sexual activity, fertility history, pregnancy status or other health payloads.

Optional encrypted continuity planned for a future release

Sreva's approved cross-platform architecture includes an optional future account mode for end-to-end encrypted continuity across Android, iOS and Web/PWA. That feature is not active in the current Android v1 runtime described by this policy source. Before a release enables it, this policy and applicable store disclosures must be updated to describe the exact behavior.

The approved architecture requires health content to be encrypted on the user's authorized device before synchronization. Sreva infrastructure may then store ciphertext and minimum operational metadata, but must not possess the key needed to read the reproductive-health vault. Account recovery through email or SMS must not by itself decrypt old health history. Account-free core health functionality must remain available.

Children and age eligibility

Sreva v1 is intended for adults. The publisher should not market the initial worldwide release as a child-directed service without a separate privacy, legal and product review.

Changes to this policy

Material privacy changes must ship only after reviewed implementation, verification and corresponding store/privacy documentation. Sreva will not silently introduce a plaintext or developer-decryptable reproductive-health database while continuing to make the local-sovereign privacy promise. If optional E2EE continuity is introduced, the policy effective for that release will explain server-visible metadata, ciphertext retention/deletion, account identifiers, trusted devices and recovery boundaries.

Contact

The current developer and publisher contact details are provided in Sreva's official Google Play or App Store listing. Privacy and security questions can be sent using that verified store contact channel.
